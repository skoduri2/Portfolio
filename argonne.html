Wildlife Detection on NSF Sage

GitHub repository ↗

During the Argonne Sage program, I worked on an edge-AI wildlife monitoring system intended to do more than simply draw a bounding box around an animal. The pipeline first detects an animal in a camera frame, then identifies the species and evaluates the potential risk of the encounter. In the version shown above, the system detects a leopard, tracks it as a persistent object, and produces an annotated output that includes identity confidence and a danger score.

The system combines YOLO11 for detection, BioCLIP 2 for species identification, and Gemma 3 for contextual risk scoring. I also developed multi-frame tracking so the same animal could be followed across successive images without generating duplicate alerts. To run the workflow on NSF Sage edge-computing infrastructure, the application was containerized with Docker and integrated with PyWaggle and Sage job manifests. The deployment handles image ingestion, structured detection publishing, persistent tracker state, and fault-tolerant storage of CSV results and annotated images.
